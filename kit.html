DIT Notion Kit

The business side of the job · a companion to DIT Media Calculator

Silverstack offloads and verifies. Parashoot decides when a card is safe to wipe. Canister catalogues the tape. None of them knows what the job pays, whose drive is in the back of your car tonight, or which invoice is three weeks late. That is what this is for.

One page per job

What this job cost you, what you are owed, and where your kit is.

Enquiry to paid: held dates, confirmed days, overtime, the kit that went out with it at its rental rate, the invoice, and how overdue it is. The footage record sits alongside it — imported, never typed.

It does not touch your footage workflow

On purpose. Those tools are good and you already own them.

  • Offload, checksums, MHL — ShotPut Pro, Silverstack, Hedge, YoYotta.
  • Deciding a card is safe to wipe — Parashoot, which enforces it physically. A checkbox in a database cannot, and should not pretend to.
  • Tape catalogues and finding a clip years later — Canister, YoYottaID, myLTO.
  • Looks, scopes, QC — Silverstack.

The roll record comes in from the ASC MHL or CSV your offload software already wrote — roll, file count, size, hash method, date. You are never asked to retype a checksum, and there is no second opinion about whether a card is backed up.

What is in it

  • Jobs — enquiry · hold · confirmed · shooting · wrapped · invoiced · paid, on one board.
  • Rates — day, prep and kit rental, per job, in the currency that job pays in.
  • Invoices — issued, due, paid, and days overdue calculated for you.
  • Kit list — serials, insurance values, service dates, and the rental rate that feeds the invoice.
  • Drives — not which volume holds which clip, but who physically has it, where it went, and whether it came back.
  • Look approvals — which LUT or CDL the DP signed off, and when. The record, not the grading.
  • Rolls — imported from MHL or CSV, one row per roll.
  • Contacts & delivery specs — who pays late, and what each production wants delivered.
  • Season view — holds and confirmed days on one calendar, so a double-booking is visible before it happens.

Offline, honestly

Notion works offline — you can view, edit and create pages on desktop and mobile without a connection. It has to be prepared first, and the limits are worth knowing before you rely on it in a basement.

  • Marked per page, per device. Turn on “Available offline” for what you need, on the machine and the phone. Do it in prep, not on the day.
  • A database downloads its first 50 rows. Beyond that you mark rows individually — so the kit’s on-set views are filtered to the job in front of you, which keeps what you need inside that window.
  • Buttons, forms and embeds do not work offline. Nothing here depends on them to capture anything.
  • Text merges on reconnect; non-text changes can want a quick look.

It is still designed to be filled at wrap and in the truck rather than tapped through during a take, and most of the footage side arrives by import rather than typing. But “no signal” is no longer the same as “no tool”.
